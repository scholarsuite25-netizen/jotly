# 09 — UX and Accessibility Requirements

## 1. Experience principles
- Responsive desktop, tablet, and mobile web experience.
- Plain language for consent, AI-generated content, deletion, and sharing.
- Recording state must always be unmistakable.
- Make source content and AI-generated interpretation visually distinct.
- Use progressive disclosure for advanced enterprise settings.
- Show processing progress and recoverable error states.
- Never imply that diarisation has verified a speaker's identity.

2. Dashboard
3. Meeting list and meeting workspace

14. Collaboration and notifications
15. Organisation administration
16. Security, audit, retention, and legal hold

## 3. Recorder interaction
- Start, pause, resume, stop.
- Input selection and level meter.
- Visible elapsed time and recording state.
- Consent reminder and notice.
- Warn before leaving the recording screen.
- Recover safely after refresh/network loss where possible.
- Distinguish recording, paused, uploading, processing, failed, and saved.

## 4. Transcript editor
- Speaker labels and timestamps.
- Segment editing, find/replace, undo.
- Revision history.
- Clear distinction between original ASR text and human correction.
- Keyboard-friendly navigation.
- Export preview.

## 5. Voice player
- Play/pause, seek, speed control, skip intervals.
- Chapter navigation where available.
- Read-along highlighting when alignment data exists.
- Keyboard shortcuts and accessible labels.
- Clear synthetic/clone disclosure.

## 6. Accessibility baseline
- Keyboard navigation and visible focus.
- Screen-reader labels and semantic structure.
- Captions/transcripts for audio content.
- Accessible contrast.
- Do not rely on colour alone to communicate state.
- Test with supported screen readers and keyboard-only workflows.
